# Hotel PMS vendor billing: pricing models, plans, add-ons, entitlements and the billing stack behind them

Research date: 2026-11-22. All web pages were accessed on 2026-11-22 unless another date is given. Labels used below: **[Vendor]** means the figure is on the vendor's own page. **[3P]** means a third-party directory, aggregator or competitor, so treat it as an estimate. **[Calc]** means arithmetic I did on cited figures.

## 1. Pricing units and typical price points (per room, per property, per booking, tiers, add-ons, marketplace fees, setup fees; USD and INR)

### Takeaway
Hotel PMS SaaS is almost always priced **per room (or unit/listing) per month, with a monthly floor per property**. Modules (RMS, POS, booking engine, kiosk, guest messaging) are sold as add-ons, and payments are a separate per-transaction line. Apaleo is the only Western vendor studied that publishes a hard price: €8 per room per month, €400 minimum, no setup fee. Cloudbeds, Mews, Hostaway, Stayntouch and Hotelogix sell by quote. The Indian and SMB-focused vendors (eZee, Djubo) use **flat monthly plans keyed to room bands**, typically about US$25–225 a month, with discounts for quarterly or annual billing.

- €8 per room per month, "Starting at €400 per month", "No setup fees · No license fees · No maintenance fees · No integration fees". Its 11 APIs are included "at no extra cost". Apaleo Pay is "Starting at 0.6%". The Store lists 200+ apps, with app pricing set by each app's vendor. Contract terms are "flexible" with "no vendor lock-in", and there is a "Try for free" option of unstated length — [Apaleo pricing](https://apaleo.com/pricing) [Vendor]

### Inferences
- **The dominant unit is "room-month with a property floor"**: Apaleo €400, Mews about $300 (estimate), Hostaway about $160 (estimate), Stayntouch $550 (estimate). The floor recovers onboarding and support cost on small hotels. A per-booking unit did not appear for any core PMS studied. Per-transaction pricing shows up only in payments.
- In India the flat room-band tier (eZee, Djubo) suits independents, whose budget is roughly ₹2,000–8,000 a month. [Calc] At ₹2,250 a month for a roughly 10-room property, that is about ₹225 (about US$2.7) per room per month. Western per-room pricing ($8–15) is about 3–5x higher.
- Discounts for quarterly or annual billing (Djubo: about 10% off monthly for annual) and perpetual licences (eZee) are India-market traits. A billing system for India needs annual prepay and quarterly cycles, not just monthly.
- Setup fees are increasingly waived as a marketing point (Apaleo "no setup fees"). Implementation cost moves to partners or is recovered through the floor.

### Gaps
- No verified official price for Cloudbeds, Mews, Hostaway, Hotelogix or Djubo. All their figures are third-party, and several come from a competitor (Roommaster), which has an incentive to frame rivals as expensive.
- eZee's official pricing page (ezeeabsolute.com/pricing.php) returned a redirect and could not be read, so the INR figures come only from Indian third-party directories.
- I found no published setup or onboarding fee schedule for any vendor other than Apaleo's "none".
- No Hotel Tech Report pricing guide was retrieved.

## 2. Payments as revenue (Cloudbeds Payments, Mews Payments, Apaleo Pay; payfac economics, bundling)

### Takeaway
Embedded payments now generate **more revenue than software** at the largest cloud PMS. In 2025 Mews earned about **75% of its €291M revenue from transactions** (€219M), against about €62M from SaaS. Most of that is passed through to gateways and card schemes (€172M), so payments gross margin is thin, but payments still carry the business. Cloudbeds (Stripe Connect, then Adyen) and Apaleo (Adyen) follow the same payfac/platform model, pricing either a blended rate or interchange plus a markup.

### Cited Findings
- **Mews 2025:** revenue grew 39% to €291M ($329M). Transaction revenue was €219M ($247M), about 75%, and core SaaS was about €62M ($70M). Transaction volume was €17B ($19.2B). External gateway commissions, including interchange and bank fees, were about €172M. Gross profit was €72M, operating loss €111M and operating cash outflow €78M. The profitability target has moved from 2024–25 to 2027 and now to 2028 — [Skift, 2026-10-06](https://skift.com/2026/10/06/inside-mews-2-5-billion-valuation-can-the-economics-catch-up/)
- Mews reported $19.7B in platform transaction volume for 2025 — [tech.eu, 2026-01-22](https://tech.eu/2026/01/22/mews-raises-300m-to-accelerate-ai-powered-hospitality-operations/). This conflicts slightly with Skift's €17B ($19.2B).
- Mews raised a $300M Series D (EQT Growth lead; Atomico and HarbourVest) at a $2.5B valuation in January 2026, earmarked for AI and the expansion of Mews Payments and fintech — [Skift, 2026-01-22](https://skift.com/2026/01/22/mews-raises-300-million-series-d-2-5-billion-valuation/); [Vestbee](https://vestbee.com/insights/articles/mews-raises-300-m). An earlier $75M round led by Tiger Global closed in March 2025 — [TechCrunch, 2025-03-04](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/04/hotel-management-platform-mews-books-75m-round-led-by-tiger-global)
- [Calc] Mews gross transaction take is €219M ÷ €17B ≈ **1.29%** of volume. After €172M of gateway costs, the net take is about €47M ÷ €17B ≈ **0.28%**. Net payments contribution (about €47M) is therefore *smaller* than SaaS revenue (€62M), even though payments make up 75% of the top line.
- **Cloudbeds Payments pricing models** [Vendor help centre]. *Cost+*: interchange plus card-network fees plus a fixed Cloudbeds markup. The example markup is 1.2%, so a domestic card with 0.8% passthrough totals 2.0% and an international card with 3.0% passthrough totals 4.2%. *Blended*: one rate per card category, with example rates of 2.9% domestic and 3.9% international, or 2.9% + $0.30 for standard cards plus 1% for international or specialty cards. A fixed per-transaction fee (about $0.30–0.35, or €0.35) varies by region. Cost+ fees are estimated at the time of the transaction and reconciled the next month as "Fees Owed" — [Cloudbeds: Cost+ vs Blended](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/14278190436507-Cloudbeds-Payments-Cost-Versus-Blended-Rate-Pricing)
- Cloudbeds built embedded payments on Stripe Connect. Stripe's case study says properties using it saw faster revenue growth and larger average tickets — [Stripe customer story](https://stripe.com/customers/cloudbeds). Cloudbeds also has Adyen-based payment terms — [Cloudbeds Payments Adyen terms](https://www.cloudbeds.com/terms/cloudbeds-payments/adyen/). A 2026 consultant article says Cloudbeds "controls the UX, underwriting decisions, fee structure, and fund flows" — [Merchant Cost Consulting](https://merchantcostconsulting.com/lower-credit-card-processing-fees/cloudbeds-payment-processing/) [3P]
- **Apaleo Pay** is "starting at 0.6%" — [Apaleo pricing](https://apaleo.com/pricing) [Vendor]. It runs on the Adyen Advanced flow, not Sessions — [apaleo.dev](https://apaleo.dev/guides/pay-integration/payment.html). Adyen's own pricing is a fixed processing fee plus a payment-method fee (Interchange++). Adyen for Platforms supports split payments, so the platform can take its commission — [Adyen pricing](https://www.adyen.com/en_AE/pricing); [Sharetribe on Adyen for Platforms](https://sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-payments/adyen-for-platforms-overview) [3P]
- Roommaster says Mews prices payments per transaction and separately from the PMS — [Roommaster](https://www.roommaster.com/blog/mews-pricing) [3P]

### Inferences
- Cloud PMS vendors use payments to **raise ARPU without raising the subscription price**. A blended 2.9% against an interchange of about 0.8% leaves roughly 1.2–2% gross per domestic card. A buyer-friendly Cost+ option exists because sophisticated hotels push back on blended pricing.
- The Mews numbers show the risk: payments inflate revenue but bring thin gross margin, and Skift frames the economics as still "catching up". For a pre-scale vendor, payments are a margin add-on, not a replacement for subscription revenue.
- **India specifics:** UPI has zero MDR by RBI mandate, yet Razorpay still charges a 2% platform fee plus GST on UPI (section 4). The payfac upside on Indian guest payments is limited, because a large share of desk payments is UPI or cash. This matches HospAI's locked decision to record UPI at the desk rather than collect it through a PSP.

### Gaps
- No verified Cloudbeds revenue or payments volume. Trackers conflict ($25M, $57M→$85M, "$1–10M"), and Cloudbeds publishes none — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/cloudbeds/); [Clay](https://www.clay.com/dossier/cloudbeds-funding).
- Whether Cloudbeds or Mews *require* their own payments (versus allowing third-party gateways at an extra fee) was not confirmed from primary sources.
- Apaleo's 0.6% basis (net or gross, which channels) is not explained on its page.

## 3. Plan and entitlement management (gating modules and limits, trials, upgrades and downgrades, proration, suspension, dunning, reactivation)

### Takeaway
PMS vendors publish almost nothing about their suspension or dunning policy. The machinery is visible mainly in the billing platforms they use. The common pattern: modules are add-ons on one monthly invoice (Mews auto-adds marketplace subscriptions), feature sets are attached to products, failed payments move through `past_due` → retries → `unpaid`/`canceled`, and access is controlled by the app's own entitlement check. India's mandate rules add a hard constraint: **recurring-mandate amounts cannot simply be modified mid-cycle without re-authentication**, so upgrades on UPI AutoPay or e-mandates need a new or re-authorised mandate.

### Cited Findings
- **Mews:** marketplace add-ons with a subscription fee are automatically added to the monthly invoice, so add-on billing is consolidated through the PMS — [Mews Help](https://help.mews.com/s/article/connect-and-disconnect-integrations). Partner fees and changes are governed by "Integration Specific Terms" — [Mews Marketplace terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/mews-marketplace-terms)
- **Trials:** Apaleo offers "Try for free" — [Apaleo](https://apaleo.com/pricing). Djubo has a free trial and no free tier — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/143927/Djubo/pricing/). Cloudbeds has no free version or trial (per TrustRadius) — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/cloudbeds/pricing). Hostaway turns away single-listing hosts (waitlist) — [Hostaway](https://www.hostaway.com/pricing-info)
- **Contract terms:** Apaleo advertises "flexible contract terms" and "no vendor lock-in" — [Apaleo](https://apaleo.com/pricing). eZee offers monthly, yearly and one-time perpetual licences — [Techimply](https://www.techimply.com/profile/ezee-absolute) [3P]
- **Stripe Entitlements:** a Feature is attached to a Product, and the customer's Active Entitlements are listed by customer ID. Stripe suggests checking on app startup, for authorisation, or to reconcile after a missed webhook, and recommends persisting entitlements internally and listening to the `entitlements.active_entitlement_summary.updated` webhook. **Existing subscriptions pick up product feature changes only at the start of the next billing period** — [Stripe Entitlements docs](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/entitlements); [Active Entitlement API](https://docs.stripe.com/api/entitlements/active-entitlement)
- **Stripe dunning lifecycle:** Stripe runs retry logic, dunning and status transitions. `past_due` means the latest invoice failed, and the subscription keeps creating invoices. When retries are exhausted, the subscription can be set to `canceled`, `unpaid` or left `past_due`. Paying the latest invoice, or marking it uncollectible, returns it to `active`. Smart Retries or a custom schedule are available. There are no retries on a hard decline or when no payment method exists. `invoice.payment_failed` is the webhook to watch — [Stripe: How subscriptions work](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/overview); [Stripe Smart Retries](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/smart-retries). Stripe can also pause subscriptions on payment failure — [Stripe pause on failure](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/revenue-recovery/smart-retries/pause-on-payment-failure)
- Third-party guidance: run Smart Retries for about 2 weeks, then mark the subscription `unpaid` — [Churn Buster](https://churnbuster.io/docs/recommended-stripe-billing-settings/) [3P vendor]
- **India mandate constraints:** under RBI's *Digital Payments – E-mandate Framework, 2026* (21 April 2026), which covers cards, PPIs and UPI, the first transaction needs AFA (additional factor of authentication). Recurring debits up to **₹15,000 per transaction** go without AFA, and anything above needs AFA. **Modifying or withdrawing a mandate needs AFA by the issuer.** Insurance, mutual-fund and credit-card bill payments may go up to ₹1,00,000 without AFA — [RBI Master Direction](https://rbi.org.in/scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13374); [Business Today, 2026-04-21](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/rbi-caps-recurring-payments-at-rs15000-without-otp-under-new-e-mandate-framework-526759-2026-04-21). History: the limit went from ₹5,000 to ₹15,000 in June 2022, and the ₹1 lakh categories were added in December 2023 — [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/article/finance/new-e-mandate-guidelines-rbi-enhances-limit-for-e-mandates-on-credit-debit-cards-to-rs-15-000-122060800417_1.html)
- Razorpay: "updates are not supported for UPI AutoPay or eMandate subscriptions" — reported in a search summary of Razorpay documentation (not fetched directly; see [Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing) and its Subscriptions docs).

### Inferences
- **Proration and upgrades:** with Stripe or Chargebee on cards, proration is a native feature (an upgrade charges a prorated invoice immediately). With Indian recurring mandates, an upgrade that raises the debit above the mandate's maximum amount, or above ₹15,000, triggers re-authentication. Practical patterns: set the mandate's max amount above the plan price at signup, bill upgrades as a one-off charge, or cancel and re-create the subscription. *(Design inference; not confirmed from a vendor doc.)*
- **Monthly PMS fees above ₹15,000** (for example a 60-room hotel on a premium tier) will need AFA on **every** debit, which kills unattended autopay. B2B Indian SaaS therefore often falls back to invoice plus bank transfer (NEFT/RTGS), or to e-NACH (bank mandates are governed by NPCI NACH, not the card/UPI AFA cap; *unverified in this session*).
- **Entitlement source of truth:** Stripe itself recommends persisting entitlements locally. The billing system should push entitlement changes into the app, and the app should enforce them from its own store, cached. That suits a design where the PMS keeps `entitlement_required` gating, a short permission/entitlement cache and a tenant kill-switch. Dunning state (`past_due` → grace → `unpaid`) maps onto staged degradation: banner, then read-only, then a kill-switch that blocks mutations except data export and legally required duties.
- **Hotel-specific suspension risk:** a hard lock-out of a PMS stops check-ins and breaks guest-register legal duties (for example India's Form C for foreign nationals). A read-only mode that keeps arrivals, departures and legal registers working is safer than a full lock. *(Inference; no vendor policy found.)*

### Gaps
- No public Cloudbeds, Mews, Apaleo, Stayntouch, eZee, Hotelogix or Djubo policy was found on grace periods, read-only mode, suspension timing or reactivation fees. Their master terms (for example [Mews Master T&Cs v5.0, 2025-02-26](https://www.mews.com/en/terms-conditions/master)) were not read in full.
- RBI's 24-hour pre-debit notification requirement and the e-NACH limits were not re-verified from the 2026 framework text in this session.
- Chargebee Dunning v2 specifics ([docs](https://www.chargebee.com/docs/payments/2.0/dunning/dunning-v2)) were not read.

## 4. Billing infrastructure (Stripe Billing, Chargebee, Zuora, Recurly, Maxio, Orb/Metronome), usage metering, and tax-correct invoicing (India GST, e-invoicing, reverse charge; Canada GST/HST)

### Takeaway
For a multi-tenant SaaS seller, the standard stack is a **subscription-billing engine** (Stripe Billing at 0.7% of billing volume, or Chargebee/Zuora/Recurly/Maxio for more finance control), **a metering engine for usage** (Metronome is now owned by Stripe; Orb is independent), **a tax layer**, and **a local payment rail**. India adds three hard requirements: GST invoices (18% on SaaS fees), IRP e-invoicing once turnover exceeds the threshold, and mandate-compliant recurring rails (Razorpay or Cashfree with UPI AutoPay and e-mandates). Chargebee's India product explicitly bundles Razorpay, UPI AutoPay and GST e-invoicing.

### Cited Findings
**Billing engines**
- **Stripe Billing:** pay-as-you-go is **0.7% of Billing volume**, with Smart Retries, reminders and recovery automations included. Billing volume covers transactions on or off Stripe that use Billing features (subscriptions, schedules, usage) — [Stripe Billing pricing](https://stripe.com/en-us/billing/pricing); [July 2024 changes](https://support.stripe.com/questions/july-2024-changes-to-stripe-billing). Third parties report a $620-a-month tier for up to $100k of monthly volume with 0.67% overage — [Orb blog](https://www.withorb.com/blog/stripe-billing-pricing) [3P, competitor]
- **Stripe and Metronome:** Stripe completed its acquisition of Metronome on 14 January 2026 (reported at $1B). Advanced usage-based billing now runs on Metronome and is priced separately, while basic usage features stay in Stripe's Meters API — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/4305263d-16a8-4088-9efd-0f5fc14253e5/) [3P]; [Landbase, 2026-09-03](https://www.landbase.com/blog/usage-based-billing-software) [3P]
- **Orb** focuses on B2B usage and hybrid pricing: query-based billing over raw events, pricing simulation, and invoicing and AR in one system — [Orb: Metronome vs Chargebee vs Orb](https://www.withorb.com/blog/metronome-vs-chargebee-vs-orb) [3P, vendor]
- **Chargebee** combines subscription lifecycle with native usage billing and supports 40+ payment gateways, so it is not tied to one processor. **Zuora** covers the enterprise range (subscriptions, usage, revenue recognition) and bought Togai for metering — [Landbase](https://www.landbase.com/blog/usage-based-billing-software) [3P]
- **Stigg**, an entitlements layer, positions itself as working *alongside* Stripe, Zuora, Chargebee or custom billing — [Stigg: Orb vs Metronome](https://www.stigg.io/blog-posts/orb-vs-metronome) [3P vendor]
- **Chargebee India** supports UPI AutoPay, RBI-compliant e-mandates, GST and TDS e-invoice generation, and integrates with Razorpay — [Chargebee for India](https://chargebee.com/for-india); [Chargebee subscription billing for India](https://chargebee.com/subscription-billing-for-india). The partnership was announced in April 2022 — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2022/04/chargebee-partners-with-razorpay-brings-solution-to-saas-and-subscription-businesses-in-india)

**India rails**
- **Razorpay standard:** 2% of successful domestic transactions plus 18% GST on that fee, with no setup or annual maintenance charge. UPI has zero MDR by RBI mandate, but Razorpay's 2% platform fee plus GST still applies. Subscriptions are reported at 2% + 0.99% plus GST in one Razorpay source and 0.9% in another (the sources conflict). UPI AutoPay and e-NACH are domestic-only, and pricing is custom above about ₹5 lakh of monthly GMV — [Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing); [Razorpay: SaaS TCO guide](https://razorpay.com/blog/best-payment-gateway-pricing-saas-india-tco-guide/); [Razorpay pricing explained](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/)
- Cashfree's mandate pricing was not retrieved (see Gaps).

**India GST on SaaS fees**
- **B2B foreign SaaS into India:** the Indian GST-registered recipient pays IGST under reverse charge, and the foreign supplier often needs no OIDAR registration for that stream. **B2C:** the foreign supplier must register under OIDAR (simplified) and charge IGST itself, filing GSTR-5A with a B2B/B2C split — [Treelife: OIDAR registration](https://treelife.in/legal/oidar-registration-in-india/); [IncorpX SaaS GST guide](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/saas-business-gst-guide-india); [India Briefing: OIDAR compliance](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/oidar-compliance-india-gst-registration-ntor-rules-gstr5a-digital-tax-43951.html/)
- **E-invoicing (IRP):** mandatory for B2B supplies, exports and credit/debit notes once aggregate annual turnover in any financial year exceeds **₹5 crore** — [OnlineLegalIndia](https://www.onlinelegalindia.com/blogs/e-invoicing-applicability) [3P]. One source claims the threshold dropped to ₹2 crore on 1 October 2025, which I could **not confirm** — [India Briefing SaaS GST](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/gst-compliance-for-saas-and-cloud-computing-in-india-explained-39021.html) (claim surfaced in a search summary). E-invoicing applies to domestic B2B and B2G supplies; supplies to buyers outside India fall outside the IRP requirement — [Vertex](https://www.vertexinc.com/resources/resource-library/indias-e-invoicing-regulations-explained-scope-formats-and-penalties)
- **30-day reporting rule:** since 1 April 2025, taxpayers with AATO ≥ ₹10 crore must report e-invoices to the IRP within 30 days of the invoice date, or the IRP rejects them. This covers credit and debit notes too. It applied to AATO ≥ ₹100 crore from 1 November 2023 — [GSTN advisory (MahaGST mirror)](https://www.mahagst.gov.in/public/uploads/gstnadvisory/1760531580_293%20Advisory%20Time%20Limit%20for%20Reporting%20e%20Invoice%20on%20the%20IRP%20Portal%20%20Lowering%20of%20Threshold%20to%20AATO%2010%20Crores%20and%20Above.pdf); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/time-limit-of-30-days-for-reporting-e-invoice-on-irp-portal-for-taxpayers-with-aato-of-10-crores-and-above-gstn-update/); [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/accounting/30-day-e-invoice-reporting-limit-impact-on-itc-buyers-and-sellers/)
- **Rate:** GST of 18% applies to payment-platform fees, as on Razorpay's fee — [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/). I did not fetch a primary source stating 18% for SaaS subscriptions generally (see Gaps).

**Canada GST/HST**
- Non-resident vendors over **C$30,000** of specified supplies to Canadian consumers in 12 months must register under the simplified regime and collect GST/HST on **B2C** sales only. For B2B, a buyer with a valid GST/HST number self-assesses (reverse charge). Vendors should collect and keep the customer's GST/HST number. Simplified registrants cannot claim input tax credits. Registration is due within 30 days of crossing the threshold — [CRA: get ready to register](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy-gsthst/register-get-ready.html); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/blog/canada-requires-non-resident-vendors-and-marketplaces-to-collect-gst-as-of-july-1.html); [BDO](https://bdo.ca/insights/Digital-sales-tax-in-Canada)

### Inferences
- **For an India-first PMS selling to hotels (B2B, domestic):** each invoice needs GSTIN capture, place-of-supply logic (CGST+SGST within a state, IGST between states), the SAC code, 18% GST, and IRP e-invoice generation (IRN plus signed QR) once turnover crosses ₹5 crore, with the 30-day upload rule from ₹10 crore. Credit notes for proration or downgrades must also be e-invoiced. Stripe Billing has no native IRP e-invoicing, so an Indian seller using Stripe would need a GSP/ASP connector. Chargebee India advertises it natively. *(Stripe gap inferred from the absence of an India e-invoice feature in the sources found; not explicitly confirmed.)*
- **Selling abroad** (for example to Canadian hotels): B2B sales are reverse-charged, so the main task is capturing and validating the buyer's tax number. The simplified regime only bites on consumer sales.
- **Usage metering** matters only if a usage unit is adopted (bookings, SMS or WhatsApp sends, AI calls, payments). With room-month pricing, a plain subscription engine with "quantity = rooms" is enough. Metronome or Orb become relevant only for consumption add-ons (for example the cost of AI copilot calls).
- **Build vs buy:** cost for 1,000 Indian hotels at ₹4,000 a month ≈ ₹4 crore a year of billing volume. [Calc] Stripe Billing at 0.7% ≈ ₹2.8 lakh a year, on top of gateway fees. Razorpay Subscriptions (about 0.9–0.99% plus the 2% gateway fee) costs more per rupee but includes mandate handling.

### Gaps
- Chargebee, Recurly and Maxio list prices were not retrieved, and neither was Zuora's (enterprise, quote-based).
- Cashfree Subscriptions pricing and mandate features were not retrieved.
- No primary CBIC notification was fetched confirming the ₹5 crore e-invoicing threshold, the unconfirmed ₹2 crore change, or the SAC code and rate for SaaS subscriptions.
- Which billing stack each PMS vendor actually uses internally to bill hotels (for example whether Mews or Cloudbeds use Zuora, Chargebee or in-house) was not found publicly.

## 5. Revenue metrics a PMS vendor tracks (MRR/ARR, NRR, churn, ARPU per room, payment take-rate)

### Takeaway
Public hotel PMS numbers are scarce. Mews' 2025 accounts (via Skift) are the best disclosure available: €62M SaaS, €219M transactions, about 1.3% gross and about 0.3% net payment take, and a 25% blended gross margin. Yanolja's Enterprise Solutions segment, which owns eZee, did KRW 352.6B (US$245.8M) in 2025, up 21%. General private-SaaS benchmarks put median NRR at about 101–108% and gross retention at about 90%.

- The metric set a PMS vendor should run: **MRR/ARR split into subscription and payments net revenue** (Mews shows why gross payments revenue misleads), **ARPU per room-month** and per property, **rooms under contract** (a better unit than "customers", since property size varies 10x), **payments penetration** (share of properties on embedded payments), **gross and net take rate**, **logo churn and gross revenue retention**, **NRR** (expansion from add-ons and room growth), and **days-sales-outstanding / dunning recovery rate** (critical in India, where invoice-and-transfer is common).

## 6. Partner revenue share for marketplace apps

### Takeaway
PMS marketplaces publish no revenue-share percentages. Mews and Apaleo let partners set their own app prices, with Mews billing paid add-ons on the hotel's PMS invoice under per-integration terms. The clearest public benchmark from adjacent platforms is Shopify: **0% on a developer's first US$1M of lifetime app revenue, then 15%**, with a 2.9% processing fee.

### Cited Findings
- **Mews:** partners pay "Fees" for integration access, detailed in Integration Specific Terms. The Marketplace terms v2.0 take effect 1 October 2026 — [Mews Marketplace terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/mews-marketplace-terms). Paid add-ons are added automatically to the hotel's Mews invoice — [Mews Help](https://help.mews.com/s/article/connect-and-disconnect-integrations). A third-party source says the developer and certification programme is free, and that Open API access (Connector, Channel Manager, Booking Engine APIs) is bundled into the Enterprise tier — [apis.io](https://apis.io/plans/mews-systems/mews-systems-plans-pricing/) [3P, low reliability]
- **Apaleo:** the Store has 200+ apps, priced by each app vendor, and APIs are free to the hotel. No partner revenue share is published — [Apaleo pricing](https://apaleo.com/pricing)
- **Shopify (cross-industry benchmark):** from 16 June 2025, the $1M threshold is **lifetime**, not annual (revenue before 1 January 2025 does not count). Rates are 0% up to $1M and 15% above, for developers under $20M of app revenue and $100M of company revenue. Larger developers pay 15% on everything. The share is computed on gross sales, there is a 2.9% processing fee, and a one-time $19 partner fee — [Shopify revenue share docs](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/launch/distribution/revenue-share); [Shopify changelog](https://shopify.dev/changelog/update-to-shopifys-app-developer-revenue-share)
- **Adyen partner models:** Adyen publishes commercial models for tech partners, and its platform split payments let a platform take a commission on payments made through partner apps — [Adyen partner commercials](https://help.adyen.com/knowledge/partners/commercials/what-are-your-commercial-models-for-partners); [Sharetribe](https://sharetribe.com/academy/marketplace-payments/adyen-for-platforms-overview) [3P]

### Inferences
- Two PMS marketplace models are visible. **(a) Open API, partner bills the hotel directly** (Apaleo-style; the PMS earns through API-driven room growth and payments). **(b) PMS bills on behalf of partners** (Mews add-ons on the PMS invoice), which implies a reseller or revenue-share agreement and needs billing that supports third-party line items, partner payouts and per-partner tax treatment. In India, (b) makes the PMS the supplier of record for GST on partner fees unless it is structured as an agent.
- Charging partners for API access or certification ("Fees") is the third lever. It is reportedly common among legacy PMS vendors, but unverified here.

### Gaps
- No PMS vendor (Cloudbeds, Mews, Apaleo, Stayntouch, eZee, Hotelogix, Djubo) publishes a marketplace revenue-share percentage or a per-connection fee. Mews' Integration Specific Terms and Cloudbeds' partner agreement were not public in the sources found.
- No sources were found on Hostaway's marketplace economics.
