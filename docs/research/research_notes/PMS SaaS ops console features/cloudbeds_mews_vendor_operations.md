# Cloudbeds and Mews vendor-side operations (with Apaleo, OPERA Cloud, Stayntouch, Hotelogix, eZee as secondary)

Research date: 2026-11-24. Method note for the report writer: pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises, so quoted phrases below are the tool's relay of the page text; re-open the source before printing any phrase as a verbatim quote. Several Mews legal pages show a bracketed effective date ("[1st October 2026]"), which I report as shown. "Documented" items are under Cited Findings; everything under Inferences is my reasoning, not vendor documentation. No vendor publishes a description of its internal admin console; that absence is itself a finding.

## 1. Tenant lifecycle: creation, configuration, training, go-live, suspension, cancellation, export and deletion

### Takeaway
Both vendors sell onboarding as a packaged, staged service with a named vendor contact and a formal pre-go-live audit, while pushing configuration work and data accuracy onto the hotel; Mews has turned the audit into an in-product "Onboarding dashboard" with pass/fail checks that the vendor's onboarding managers also use. Suspension for non-payment is contractual and fast (Mews: more than 7 days late; Cloudbeds: immediate at its discretion, plus a USD 500 reinstatement fee), and neither vendor publishes a day-count export or deletion window after termination.

### Cited Findings

Mews, onboarding packages and division of labour
- Onboarding Terms v2.0 (effective 1 October 2025; v1.0 from 24 February 2025 for earlier signers) define three packages: Classic (structured pathway, digital resources, Mews University, remote support during onboarding and 30 days after go-live), Classic Plus (Classic plus a series of remote calls with a dedicated Onboarding Manager), Custom (remote design sessions with an Onboarding Manager, 30 days post-go-live support), plus on-site add-ons of 2 or 4 days — [Mews Onboarding Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/onboarding-terms)
- For all packages the Partner (hotel) is responsible for "system configuration, integration connectivity, and reservation imports" and has "sole responsibility" for accuracy of setup and imported data; only under Custom may Mews adjust the system, with the Partner's cooperation — [Mews Onboarding Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/onboarding-terms)
- Help-centre articles list Classic at 1 to 3 weeks to go-live with 30 days of intensive support and Custom at 5 to 7 weeks — [Classic package](https://help.mews.com/s/article/What-is-the-Classic-onboarding-package-for-Mews-Operations), [Custom package](https://help.mews.com/s/article/What-is-the-Custom-onboarding-package-for-Mews-Operations). A Mews blog gives a different average of about two months and says one-week go-lives have happened — [Mews blog](https://www.mews.com/en/blog/onboarding-new-property-management-system). These figures conflict; package naming also conflicts across pages (the audit article still says Standard / Premium / Digital Onboarding).
- The Onboarding dashboard is a feature inside Mews Operations (Settings > Property > Onboarding dashboard) that guides setup and flags missing requirements with statuses Pass, Fail, Review ("flagged for a human to check") and Pending; Onboarding Managers use the same checks to audit the configuration — [Mews Onboarding System Audit](https://help.mews.com/s/article/Mews-Onboarding-System-Audit)
- A passing audit requires: Mews University courses assigned to each user, platform configuration, channel manager testing scheduled for go-live day, KYB verification, and settlement of pending Mews subscription invoices — [Mews Onboarding System Audit](https://help.mews.com/s/article/Mews-Onboarding-System-Audit)
- For Standard and Premium packages a Mews Onboarding Manager runs the audit; under Digital Onboarding the property requests the audit from the dashboard once no mandatory task is "Fail". The audit takes up to three business days. Outcome is GO or NO GO; on NO GO the property cannot run on Mews or enable distribution integrations (booking engine, channel managers). On failure Mews moves go-live back at least two weeks and schedules a new Go/No Go call seven days before the new date — [Mews Onboarding System Audit](https://help.mews.com/s/article/Mews-Onboarding-System-Audit)
- The hotel sets and edits its go-live date in the dashboard; Friday, Saturday and Sunday cannot be selected, and "the onboarding team uses it to allocate resources" — [Mews Onboarding System Audit](https://help.mews.com/s/article/Mews-Onboarding-System-Audit)
- Go-live day tasks for properties changing PMS: set up the cashier, create availability blocks (not carried by the import), import future and stayover reservations, review integrations and the channel manager; Mews recommends checking all reservations for the next 5 to 10 days — [Go-live tasks](https://help.mews.com/s/article/what-are-the-go-live-tasks-for-properties-changing-pms)
- Mews job posting (Customer Onboarding Manager, US, posted 18 August 2026): owns the onboarding journey for medium and high-value clients including enterprise groups, delivers training by webcast/video/online classroom, uses AI tools to build training material and draft client communications — [Mews job via Atomico board](https://careers.atomico.com/companies/mews-2/jobs/90281790-customer-onboarding-manager). Another 2026 posting says the role will "configure the product for each customer, diagnose integration issues", and run several projects at once within SLA targets including phased multi-property rollouts — [Mews careers](https://www.mews.com/en/careers/jobs/5032088101) (search snippet only, not opened).
- Mews Professional Services Terms v1.0 (24 April 2026) add a separate paid, project-based service line: consulting, optimisation, technical advisory, implementation, system audits, configuration reviews; deliverables deemed accepted after ten business days; Mews may use subcontractors — [Mews Professional Services Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/professional-services-terms-and-conditions)
- Mews' sub-processor page says certified implementation partners may access hotel data for deployment or configuration — [Mews Sub-Processors](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/mews-sub-processors)

Cloudbeds, onboarding
- Seven stages: prepare for kickoff; kickoff call with a designated Onboarding Coach; setup and configuration (property team completes assigned tasks between sessions and updates a checklist); audit (property and room setup, rates, policies, taxes, booking engine and channel access, reservation flow, integrations); go-live; hypercare (first 10 days, with Day 2 and Day 9 check-ins); adoption and optimisation — [Cloudbeds onboarding](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041220954-What-to-expect-during-Cloudbeds-onboarding)
- The Coach "guides implementation, provides training and best practices"; the property team "completes configuration tasks"; a 24/7 team is available after go-live; Adoption is only in the Professional and Complete packages — [Cloudbeds onboarding](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041220954-What-to-expect-during-Cloudbeds-onboarding)
- Cloudbeds job titles visible in 2026 listings: Onboarding Project Manager (NORAM), Hypercare Representative (global and LATAM), Onboarding Guest Experience Coordinator, Data Services Analyst, Advanced Support Specialist (booking engine and connectivity), Advanced Support Lead, Senior Support Operations Manager, Associate Billing Specialist — [search results listing Cloudbeds postings](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/cloudbeds/jobs/4697370005), [Builtin: Onboarding Project Manager](https://www.builtinaustin.com/job/onboarding-project-manager-noram/9646554), [Builtin: Associate Billing Specialist](https://builtin.com/job/associate-billing-specialist/3480338) (titles from search listings; bodies not all opened; the Hypercare listing returned HTTP 410).

Suspension, termination, export, deletion
- Mews Master Terms v6.0 ("[1st October 2026]", applies to Partners signing on or after that date): Mews may suspend when payment is more than seven days past due (§7(a)(i)) or on material breach (§7(a)(ii)); it must notify the Partner promptly and restore access promptly after cure (§7(c)); it may refuse, suspend or terminate payment processing if KYC cannot be completed or maintained (§2(d)); termination for breach needs a 30-day cure period (§5(b)); fees for the full term remain payable on other terminations (§5(e)) — [Mews Master Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/mews-master-terms-and-conditions)
- Mews DPA v3.5: Mews returns personal data "by enabling the Partner to download it" before the end of the term, then deletes it unless law requires retention; no day count is stated (§3.6) — [Mews DPA](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/data-processing-addendum)
- Cloudbeds Master Terms (page says it was archived on 30 September 2026 and points to a replacement, so confirm the governing version): non-payment of undisputed fees may bring "immediate suspension or termination of the Platform" (§6.3); suspension "without further notice" for a significant threat to functionality, security, integrity or availability (§5.3); suspension during an ownership dispute until it is resolved (§8.4); termination for breach after 30 days to cure, or 5 business days for undisputed non-payment (§8.3); late interest of the lesser of 1.5% per month, a USD 500 reinstatement fee, USD 100 per failed payment reprocessing, USD 100 admin fee for invoicing changes (§6.2.6, §6.3); confidential information (which includes Customer Data) returned or destroyed within 30 days if directed (§8.5) — [Cloudbeds Master Terms](https://www.cloudbeds.com/master-terms-2026/)
- Cloudbeds DPA: during the term the client can access, retrieve or delete data through the services (§6.1); on termination Cloudbeds must delete client personal data "in accordance with the Documentation", no deadline given, with backup and legal retention exceptions (§6.2) — [Cloudbeds DPA](https://www.cloudbeds.com/terms/dpa/)
- Cloudbeds Websites terms mention a suspended account being reactivated "within 6 months from the time of suspension" and state that site export is not supported — [Cloudbeds Websites terms](https://www.cloudbeds.com/terms/websites/)

Secondary vendors
- Apaleo: a property is created in Test status; while in Test it can be reset, deleting all transactional data (reservations, folios, payments, charges) while units, unit groups and rate plans remain; going live is a switch in Settings > Property; the API has a reset endpoint and a "move property to live" action — [apaleo help centre](https://apaleo.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4403408049554) (search snippet; the page returned 403 to my fetch, and the API detail comes from third-party mirrors).
- OPERA Cloud: "Properties are provisioned on activation of the ... OPERA Cloud Service, Property Management Professional Foundation or ... Enterprise Foundation subscription"; the customer then edits General Information and Property Controls, including a Begin Date and End Date for when the property is active — [Oracle: Configuring Properties](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/24.1/ocsuh/t_admin_configuring_properties.htm). OPERA Cloud onboarding depends on the customer first activating its OCI tenancy for identity — [Oracle OCIM guide](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/identity-management/23.1.1/ocimy/ch_creating_oci_iam_identity_domain.htm)
- Stayntouch standard onboarding: about 6 to 8 weeks of prep before cutover; kickoff is scheduled only after the first implementation invoice is paid; named roles are Project Manager, Implementation Consultant and Transition/Client Success Manager; the property names a "Property Champion" who owns configuration deliverables; the vendor confirms the nightly batch time 10 days before go-live (default 2am local); the Transition Manager supports the property for 60 days after go-live, then hands over to Support with a Support Guide and helpdesk access — [Stayntouch onboarding outline](https://stayntouch.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/24000013054-stayntouch-pms-onboarding-detailed-outline)

### Inferences
- The lifecycle states a vendor console needs to hold per tenant are roughly: contracted, kickoff done, in setup, audit requested, audit GO / NO GO, go-live scheduled (date owned by the hotel but visible to the vendor for staffing), live, hypercare (10 to 60 days), steady state, suspended (non-payment / security / ownership dispute / KYC), reinstated, terminated, data deleted. Each of these appears in at least one vendor's public material.
- Mews' audit shows that go-live gating is a mix of machine checks (configuration completeness), human review items, and commercial gates (training assigned, KYB approved, invoices settled). A console needs a per-tenant readiness checklist with those three kinds of item and an explicit GO / NO GO record that blocks distribution features.
- Because both contracts allow suspension within days of a missed payment and Cloudbeds charges a reinstatement fee, both vendors must have a suspend / reinstate switch tied to billing status, with a customer notice. Neither says whether suspension is read-only or full lock-out.
- Cloudbeds' "ownership dispute" suspension (§8.4) and support-only change of Organization Owner imply a vendor-side ownership-transfer procedure with identity checks.

### Gaps
- No vendor publishes who physically creates the tenant record (sales ops, onboarding, or automated from the signed order). Oracle's wording ("provisioned on activation of the subscription") is the closest.
- No published export format or post-termination retrieval window for either Mews or Cloudbeds; the Mews DPA says "download" before term end, and the Cloudbeds DPA defers to "the Documentation".
- I could not open a Cloudbeds hypercare or onboarding job description (410 / index pages), so the duties of those roles are inferred from titles and the help article.
- Hotelogix and eZee/Yanolja Cloud Solution: I found only review-aggregator material (support and onboarding rated highly, free trial listed for eZee Absolute) and no primary lifecycle documentation — [HotelTechReport comparison](https://hoteltechreport.com/compare/ezee-absolute-vs-hotelogix-hms). Treat as not researched to a usable standard.

## 2. Support access: can vendor staff log in to or impersonate a customer account, with what consent and logging?

### Takeaway
Neither Mews nor Cloudbeds publicly documents a customer-controlled "grant support access" toggle, time-limited access, or a customer-visible record of vendor staff sessions; the contracts permit access for service and support purposes and the security pages claim least privilege. The only concrete public traces are that Cloudbeds Support can reset a property user's password from the Users page, that Cloudbeds has a "Super Admins" audience for the Organization Log, and that Cloudbeds Support cannot grant credit-card viewing.

### Cited Findings
- Cloudbeds Master Terms §5.2: the company will not access Customer Data except to provide the services, to "prevent or address service or technical problems", or "at Customer's request in connection with customer support matters"; no separate consent step is described — [Cloudbeds Master Terms](https://www.cloudbeds.com/master-terms-2026/)
- "Property Owners and the Cloudbeds Support Team can reset the system password from the Users page"; organization users' and owners' passwords cannot be reset from that page — [Cloudbeds: users and activity log](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc//articles/221793248)
- The Organization Owner "can only be modified by our support team" — [Cloudbeds: organization users](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc//articles/115002301753-Add-or-edit-organization-users) (search snippet)
- Cloudbeds Support cannot grant credit-card viewing access; only a Property Owner can — [Cloudbeds: credit card viewing password](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc//articles/4411175776667-Grant-and-set-up-credit-card-viewing-password) (search snippet)
- Locked-out accounts require "escalation to the security team for manual unblocking" — [Cloudbeds Help Center article](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/218857877-Cloudbeds-Help-Center-Access-Support-Options-and-Ticket-Management) (search snippet)
- The Cloudbeds Activity Log records "changes made in the property by users and system events", is exportable (CSV, Excel, PDF), and keeps deactivated users labelled "Inactive"; a separate Organization Log is "available to Organization Owners, Organization Admins, and Super Admins"; neither article says whether Cloudbeds staff actions appear or how they are labelled — [Cloudbeds logs overview](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/360052062714-Logs-overview-Activity-Log-Organization-Log-and-Availability-Changes), [Cloudbeds Organizations](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/1260803386269-Organizations-Everything-you-need-to-know)
- Cloudbeds DPA Schedule 2: "Employees are only allowed access to tasks assigned to them"; access limited to applications a role requires; "We log administrator and user activities"; staff sign confidentiality commitments and receive regular privacy and security training — [Cloudbeds DPA](https://www.cloudbeds.com/terms/dpa/)
- Mews Master Terms v6.0 contain no clause on support staff access or consent; §1(b) lets Mews "host, use, transmit and display Partner Data" and §11(c)(iii) allows disclosure to employees and agents with a need to know — [Mews Master Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/mews-master-terms-and-conditions)
- Mews DPA v3.5 §3.3: "Mews shall ensure that Personnel's access to Personal Data is limited to those performing Services"; §3.1: "Mews shall keep a log of the performed Processing operations" — [Mews DPA](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/data-processing-addendum)
- Mews Platform Documentation: "We follow the principle of least privilege for all our internal systems"; MFA enforced on internal systems that support it; Azure AD SSO; Okta listed for identity management; background checks for candidates in sensitive roles; "We use multiple levels of internal system logs and provide audit log to our clients inside the platform" — [Mews Platform Documentation](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/platform-documentation)
- Mews Professional Services Terms §8.1: Mews accesses Partner systems and data only as needed for the services, by personnel or subcontractors bound by confidentiality — [Mews Professional Services Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/professional-services-terms-and-conditions)
- Mews support channel facts: support cases are visible inside Mews Operations under the profile menu (admins see all property cases, others their own); Premium Support requires a Mews Account for every user who may contact support, and contacts from unlinked addresses get standard response times; enabling identity re-verification for adding users requires contacting Mews Support; an admin who cannot log in must call the critical line — [Support cases screen](https://help.mews.com/s/article/The-Support-cases-screen-in-Mews-Operations), [Premium Support terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/premium-support), [Identity verification](https://help.mews.com/s/article/How-to-verify-your-identity-and-re-authorize-your-account-in-Mews-Operations), [Login error](https://help.mews.com/s/article/Mews-Login-error) (search snippets)
- A Mews status incident on 12 November 2026 was titled along the lines of Commander detail pages failing "affecting enterprise users opening properties that aren't their own" — [status.mews.com](https://status.mews.com/)
- Apaleo's role article describes a property-level role as suited to "general managers or internal Apaleo support roles" — [apaleo User Roles](https://apaleo.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001498771-User-Roles) (search snippet; ambiguous wording)

### Inferences
- Since Cloudbeds Support can reset passwords "from the Users page", support agents have some way to open a property's Users page. Whether that is an impersonation session, a separate staff role inside the tenant, or an internal back office mirroring the page is not stated. "Super Admins" in the Organization Log audience may be that staff role, but the PMS help centre never defines it (the only definition found is for a different product, Guest Experience, where Super Admin is a hotel GM role).
- The Mews incident wording suggests the Operations app (still called Commander internally) supports users opening enterprises other than their home one. That fits portfolio users and probably Mews staff, but the status page does not say which.
- The contractual model at both vendors is purpose limitation, not customer approval: access is allowed for providing the service and support, with no per-session consent. A product wanting to exceed this baseline could offer a customer-visible staff access log and time-boxed access, since neither incumbent advertises one.
- Clear limits that vendors do publish are instructive for console design: support can reset a password but cannot grant card-data viewing; ownership changes go through support; lock-outs go to a security team, not first-line support. That is a tiered permission model inside the vendor.

### Gaps
- No public document from Mews or Cloudbeds describes impersonation, a support-access toggle, session time limits, or how staff sessions appear in the customer's audit log. Searches of both help centres returned nothing. This is the largest evidence gap in the assignment.
- Oracle's hosting and delivery policies (which do cover Oracle staff access to customer cloud environments) were not retrieved; My Oracle Support content is gated.
- The Mews Trust Center (trust.mews.com) gates its SOC 2 report, which would describe staff access controls.

## 3. Environments: demo, sandbox, test, training, certification

### Takeaway
Mews runs four environments (production, demo, development, feature-branch) with a shared public demo for integrators and dedicated demo properties created by partner success; Cloudbeds issues partner test accounts prepopulated with dummy data on request by email. Apaleo and OPERA Cloud go further with tenant-level reset: apaleo resets transactional data on Test-status properties, and OPERA Cloud has a non-production "Refresh Property" utility that re-provisions a property from an imported configuration shell.

### Cited Findings
- Mews environments: Production, Demo, Development, Feature-branch. A daily development snapshot is deployed to demo ("feature-freeze"), then to production the next day if demo shows no problems — [Mews Platform Documentation](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/platform-documentation)
- Mews demo API addresses are api.mews-demo.com and ws.mews-demo.com; production is api.mews.com / app.mews.com. "The demo environments are completely public and NO REAL DATA should be used" — [Mews Connector API environments](https://docs.mews.com/connector-api/guidelines/environments)
- Two demo variants exist, Gross Pricing (UK tax environment) and Net Pricing (US, Washington DC), each with published shared test tokens; shared credentials can hit 429 limits when several developers test the same property — [Mews Connector API environments](https://docs.mews.com/connector-api/guidelines/environments)
- New partners complete the "Become a Mews Partner" form and get an automated password-reset email for a dedicated demo property, opted in to both gross and net; existing partners ask partner success to create users, giving demo property name, names and email — [Mews Connector API environments](https://docs.mews.com/connector-api/guidelines/environments)
- "Tokens and credentials in the Demo environment may be reset periodically" (POS API docs; not stated for the Connector API) — [Mews POS API environments](https://docs.mews.com/pos-api/guidelines/environments)
- Hotels also have a "demo Mews Operations environment" in which they can connect integrations; it does not automatically email the access token to the partner, so the hotel must send it manually — [Mews: connect an integration in demo](https://help.mews.com/s/article/how-to-connect-an-integration-in-mews-demo)
- Mews Master Terms v6.0 refer to testing "in any test environment made available by Mews" (§1(l)(ii)) and Proof of Concept access "for evaluation purposes" (§1(m)) — [Mews Master Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/mews-master-terms-and-conditions)
- Cloudbeds: a partner requests a sandbox before the partnership review; the test account is "prepopulated with dummy data"; "A Partner dev test account does not guarantee access to Cloudbeds Marketplace"; before certification an app can only connect to its test account — [Cloudbeds: getting started as a partner](https://developers.cloudbeds.com/docs/getting-started-as-a-partner-in-5-steps), [Cloudbeds Integration Guide](https://developers.cloudbeds.com/docs/integration-guide) (second is search snippet)
- A second Cloudbeds test account is requested by email to the integrations team with property name, Cloudbeds property ID and confirmation that API access is enabled; after certification up to 2 additional sets of API credentials can be offered for other environments — [Cloudbeds: property and group API access](https://developers.cloudbeds.com/docs/property-and-group-account-api-access), [Cloudbeds Integration Guide](https://developers.cloudbeds.com/docs/integration-guide)
- One Cloudbeds page describes api.cloudbeds-stage.com as a full staging environment mirroring production — [Cloudbeds API page](https://www.cloudbeds.com/?p=93749) (search snippet only; not verified by opening)
- OPERA Cloud Refresh Property: enabled in non-production environments; re-provisions a property from an imported configuration shell "from a production or other UAT environment on the same version" or from the standard seeded configuration; "No transactional data is retained"; original Property Details, Property Controls and cloud service subscriptions are restored; accessible from a hub location only; blocked if the imported property's business date is within the last three days; refresh history with downloadable error file; "Log data is purged after 30 days"; companion utilities are Export and Import Property Configuration Shells — [Oracle: Re-provisioning (Refresh) Property](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/25.1/ocsuh/t_toolbox_re-provisioning_refresh_property.htm)
- Apaleo: Test-status properties can be reset at any time, deleting transactional data and keeping setup — [apaleo help centre](https://apaleo.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/4403408049554) (search snippet). Developer accounts are free and come with Apaleo Pay pre-configured in test mode — [apaleo Pay setup guide](https://apaleo.dev/guides/pay-integration/setup.html) (search snippet)
- Stayntouch gives the property access to a training environment for practice in weeks 5 to 7 — [Stayntouch onboarding outline](https://stayntouch.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/24000013054-stayntouch-pms-onboarding-detailed-outline)

### Inferences
- Three distinct needs appear and each maps to a console capability: (a) a shared public demo with periodically rotated credentials for prospects and integrators; (b) dedicated demo or test tenants created by a partner or onboarding team from a request, seeded with dummy data; (c) reset or re-provision of a non-live tenant that wipes transactions but keeps configuration.
- OPERA's "configuration shell" export/import and apaleo's Test/Live flag are the two cleanest public models for a "tenant mode" attribute plus a guarded reset action. Both forbid the reset on live data (apaleo by status, Oracle by environment).
- Mews deploys to demo one day ahead of production, so the demo environment doubles as a release canary that partners and hotels can observe.

### Gaps
- Whether Cloudbeds gives hotels (not partners) a training or sandbox property is not documented in what I found.
- No Mews statement on how often Connector demo data or tokens are reset.
- How OPERA Cloud non-production (UAT) environments are requested and who at Oracle provisions them was not found in public docs.

## 4. Data migration and import

### Takeaway
All three vendors with documentation treat reservation import as an assisted service using spreadsheets, with the hotel responsible for data accuracy: Cloudbeds staffs a Data Services team working from CSV/Excel, Mews imports future and stayover reservations on go-live day, and Stayntouch has a named "Dataloader" import tool plus an Excel "Auto-Configurator" for setup.

### Cited Findings
- Cloudbeds offers an optional Reservation Import Service chosen during onboarding to "reduce manual work when moving reservations and guest data from your previous system" — [Cloudbeds onboarding](https://myfrontdesk.cloudbeds.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041220954-What-to-expect-during-Cloudbeds-onboarding)
- Cloudbeds Data Services Analyst (remote, Thailand; listing expired, "posted 4 months ago"): main technical advisor for clients transitioning historical data (reservations, guest profiles, inventory); "Extract, clean, map, and validate client data from CSV/Excel formats"; fix formatting problems or system mismatches before go-live; guide post-migration audits "across financial histories and occupancy metrics"; tools named are Excel and Google Sheets; experience of Opera, Mews or Protel preferred; sits with customer success — [Cloudbeds Data Services Analyst listing](https://www.workingnomads.com/jobs/data-services-analyst-cloudbeds-1712917)
- Mews: reservation imports are a Partner responsibility under the Onboarding Terms; on go-live the hotel imports future and stayover reservations; availability blocks are not transferred; Atomize RMS onboarding needs 12 months of daily data for properties with under a year on Mews — [Mews Onboarding Terms](https://www.mews.com/en/legal/onboarding-terms), [Go-live tasks](https://help.mews.com/s/article/what-are-the-go-live-tasks-for-properties-changing-pms), [Atomize onboarding](https://help.mews.com/s/article/How-to-onboard-with-Atomize-RMS-by-Mews-in-Mews-Operations) (last two are search snippets)
- Mews Multi-Property uses downloadable Excel templates per entity type to import mapping keys, with a limit of 10,000 companies per import; a "Configuration file" is received during onboarding — [Mews Multi-Property portfolio setup](https://help.mews.com/s/article/How-to-set-up-your-portfolio-using-Mews-Multi-Property)
- Stayntouch Dataloader imports company profiles, travel agency profiles, guest profiles and reservation data including advance deposit totals; the Auto-Configurator uses a standard Excel worksheet to configure the PMS, cutting "2-3 days of work for 1 full time employee" to a few hours — [Stayntouch onboarding outline](https://stayntouch.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/24000013054-stayntouch-pms-onboarding-detailed-outline)
- Stayntouch advises submitting import files in test form about a week before migration so the implementation team can run a trial import — [Stayntouch blog](https://www.stayntouch.com/blog/how-to-make-the-switch-to-saas-hotel-pms/) (search snippet)
- Cloudbeds' own implementation guide recommends a test migration on a subset and a data audit before the migration date — [Cloudbeds implementation guide](https://cloudbeds.com/hotel-management-software-guide/implementation) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- The realistic vendor-side import tool is a validate-then-load pipeline over spreadsheets: upload, map columns, dry-run with an error report, fix, load, then a post-load audit report (counts, deposits, occupancy by date). Every documented vendor process contains a trial or audit step.

### Gaps
- No public file specifications or field lists for the Cloudbeds or Mews reservation imports.
- No statement from Mews about whether its own staff run the import under Classic Plus or Custom, beyond the "Partner is responsible" clause.

- OPERA Cloud: properties are managed under Administration > Enterprise > Chain and Property; Chain Mode is read-only at property level; Refresh Property runs from a hub location; configuration shells are exported and imported between environments; with chain profile sharing, profiles are not deleted on refresh — [Oracle: Configuring Properties](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/24.1/ocsuh/t_admin_configuring_properties.htm), [Oracle: Refresh Property](https://docs.oracle.com/en/industries/hospitality/opera-cloud/25.1/ocsuh/t_toolbox_re-provisioning_refresh_property.htm)

### Inferences
- Linking properties into a group is a vendor-staff action at Cloudbeds and effectively so at Mews. A console needs create group, attach or detach property, set or change group owner, and a log of those actions (Cloudbeds' Organization Log records organisation-level changes).
- I found no Cloudbeds or Mews help article describing "copy this property's configuration to a new property". Oracle's configuration shell is the only documented copy mechanism among these vendors, and it is positioned for non-production re-provisioning. Copy-setup as a staff tool for chain rollouts therefore looks like a differentiator rather than table stakes, on the public evidence.

### Gaps
- How a new property is added to an existing Cloudbeds Organization or Mews chain (and whether billing changes automatically) is not documented publicly.
- Mews central rate inheritance rules and Cloudbeds shared guest profiles were not examined in depth.

## 10. Public descriptions of internal admin tools, back office, feature flags and tenant configuration tooling

### Takeaway
There is no public description of an internal super-admin console at any of these vendors. The evidence is indirect: named third-party tools around the edges (Salesforce, Okta, Aircall, Twilio, LaunchDarkly, Statuspage, Coralogix at Mews; Zendesk help centres, Instatus and Stripe-hosted onboarding at Cloudbeds), in-product surfaces shared between staff and customers (Mews Onboarding dashboard, support cases screen, queues), and staff-only actions revealed by "contact Support to..." phrases in help articles.

### Inferences
Consolidated list of what a vendor ops console appears to need, each item traced to the public evidence above (this whole list is inference):
- Tenant directory and lifecycle: property and group records, lifecycle state, go-live date, onboarding package, assigned onboarding contact, hypercare end date (Mews dashboard and audit; Cloudbeds seven stages; Stayntouch 60-day transition).
- Go-live readiness checklist with machine, human and commercial gates and a GO / NO GO record that blocks distribution (Mews audit).
- Group management: create group, attach properties, owner change with verification, organisation-level log (Cloudbeds Organization).
- Entitlements: plan, add-ons, support tier, API access, beta and feature-flag cohorts per tenant (Cloudbeds package gating; Mews LaunchDarkly cohorts and Beta Terms).
- Billing status and suspension: invoice state, overdue timer, suspend and reinstate with customer notice, reason codes for non-payment, security, ownership dispute, KYC (both master terms).
- Payments compliance queue: KYB/KYC state per legal entity, missing-document list, deadlines, disable and re-enable (Cloudbeds risk team; Mews KYB support).
- Support actions with tiering: password reset, lock-out release by a security role, 2FA or identity re-verification settings, with explicit exclusions such as card-data access (Cloudbeds help centre).
- Environment tools: issue demo or test tenants with seed data, rotate shared demo credentials, reset a non-live tenant, export and import configuration (Mews partner success; Cloudbeds integrations team; apaleo reset; Oracle shells).
- Data import pipeline: spreadsheet upload, mapping, dry run, error report, load, post-load audit (Cloudbeds Data Services; Stayntouch Dataloader).
- Partner and app registry: certification status, scopes, client credentials, connected properties, last-use, disable switches at app, partner and connection level (Mews Marketplace Terms; Cloudbeds developer docs).
- Integration health and message queues across tenants with replay (Mews channel manager queue and incident notes).
- Incident and communication tools: status components, affected-tenant list, postmortem link, uptime ledger for SLA credits, targeted in-app banners and release notes (Mews SLA and status page; Cloudbeds payment banners).
- Staff identity and audit: SSO, MFA, role-scoped access, logged admin actions (both DPAs; Mews platform documentation).

Other observations:
- Vendors of this size assemble the back office from commercial tools (CRM for cases and accounts, status page, flag service, payments provider onboarding) and keep a thinner in-house admin layer for tenant-specific actions. The public evidence does not show how thick that in-house layer is.
- Mews and Oracle both place operator tooling inside the main product behind permissions (Onboarding dashboard, support cases, Toolbox), rather than only in a separate console. That is a design choice a new vendor has to make deliberately.

### Gaps
- I found no Mews engineering blog post, conference talk or job advert describing an internal back office, admin panel or tenant configuration tool by name. A search of the Mews developer blog topic returned only general architecture statements.
- Cloudbeds support and onboarding job descriptions that name internal tools could not be opened (aggregator index pages, one HTTP 410); the tool list for Cloudbeds staff is therefore unknown beyond the hosted services visible from URLs.
- Whether Mews "Mews Operations" has a staff-only mode or whether staff use a separate application is unknown.
- Hotelogix and eZee/Yanolja Cloud Solution: no primary-source evidence gathered on internal tooling.
